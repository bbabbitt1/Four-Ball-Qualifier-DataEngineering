How the stats work

Every stat compares a side with others, so a 64 at a hard course in a strong field can outrank a 63.

Score vs field Field z
How many standard deviations the side's better-ball gross was below that day's field average: (gross − field mean) ÷ field std dev. −2.0 means among the very best rounds of the day. Lower is better.
Score vs field, robust
The same idea using the field median and MAD (median absolute deviation) instead of mean and std dev, so a few blow-up rounds don't make everyone else look better. Lower is better.
To-par vs season
The side's to-par as a z-score against every side that played that season, at every site. Compares across courses, but ignores how the day played. Lower is better.
Strokes from qualifying Missed by
Gross minus the highest score that qualified at the site. 0 means the side tied the number and lost the playoff, 1 means one shot out. Lower is better.
Score vs course rating
Gross minus the USGA course rating, the expected score of a scratch golfer. Better-ball scores run well under it, so compare sides with each other, not with zero. Lower is better.
WAGR strength of field WAGR SoF
How many ranked amateurs (and pros) were in the site's field, using the World Amateur Golf Ranking in effect that week. Each player is worth 1 ÷ √rank points: #1 = 1.0, #100 = 0.1, #2,500 = 0.02, unranked = 0. Pros without a WAGR rank count as #25 (50+ PGA Tour starts), #100 (any PGA Tour or 25+ Korn Ferry) or #500 (any Korn Ferry or 10+ PGA Tour Americas). Points are averaged per side, then compared with that season's other qualifiers as a z-score. Higher is a stronger field.
Field scoring strength Field to-par
The site's average to-par minus the season's average. Negative means the whole field went low that day, so getting through took a better score. Lower is tougher.
Composite
Each stat is converted to a z-score among that season's alternates and flipped so higher is always better. The composite is the weighted average of those z-scores, using the weights in the Weights panel. Weights are scaled so they don't need to total 100. A missing value counts as average (0).
Δ
Places gained or lost compared with the default weights.
Alt
The site's 1st or 2nd alternate. "?" marks the few BlueGolf sites that list two alternates tied, with no order.
